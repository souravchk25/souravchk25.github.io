What if the options form a continuum, several learners share them without talking, or the people trying them must be paid to explore?

Prices, positions and radio frequencies are continuous, and nearby choices earn similar rewards: $|\mu(x) - \mu(y)| \le L\,\|x - y\|$. Now let $N$ agents search the same space at once, with no way to talk. Two mobile sensors that come too close lose their readings to interference and learn only that they collided. We study two collision rules: agents collide when they land in the same cell of a fixed grid, or when they come within a distance $\rho$ of each other. Either way the team has to find its best placement with collisions as its only signal.

With the cell rule, the agents coordinate once and then each learns alone in its own cell; at a fixed confidence level, the cost of coordinating does not grow with the horizon. With the distance rule, they keep one shared partition, play points inside its cells that are more than $\rho$ apart, and send each other reward summaries through deliberate collisions; in the worst case the team's regret matches a centralized lower bound up to log factors. I have also worked on incentivized exploration: a principal pays short-sighted agents to explore, and the payments bias the rewards they report. We give algorithms whose regret and total payment both grow sublinearly, for continuous actions and for rewards that jump or drift.
