How much does quantum access to rewards help a bandit learner, and does the help survive when the rewards change?

In a quantum bandit the rewards are still ordinary random numbers, but the learner can also call a quantum oracle for each arm that prepares a superposition over its reward values. Quantum Monte Carlo then estimates an arm's mean to accuracy $\varepsilon$ with about $\sigma/\varepsilon$ calls, where classical sampling needs $\sigma^2/\varepsilon^2$ draws ($\sigma$ is the reward's standard deviation). Earlier work showed that with fixed rewards this lets regret grow only logarithmically in the horizon $T$. Real rewards drift, though: click-through rates follow the news cycle, and channel quality changes with interference.

Classical methods handle drift by forgetting old samples, but a coherent estimate is one measured number whose queries cannot be discounted one by one. We restart a stationary quantum learner in blocks of length $w^\star \asymp \sqrt{KT/V_T}$, the coherence window, where $V_T$ caps the total movement of the $K$ arms' means. Assuming the oracles change smoothly with the means, the dynamic regret is $\tilde O(\sqrt{K V_T T})$ instead of the classical $(K V_T)^{1/3} T^{2/3}$, a matching lower bound holds for learners that choose each queried arm classically, and a variant learns the window when $V_T$ is unknown. For fixed rewards we pin down the rate itself: learners that query one classically chosen arm at a time have minimax regret $\Theta(K\sigma\log T)$ when $\sigma$ is of constant order. The $\log T$ is the cost of making every estimate reliable, and keeping quantum memory between estimates does not remove it.
